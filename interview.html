01 · How hiring works

The rounds you'll face

Most campus hiring follows one of two patterns. Know which one your company uses before you prepare.

Service companies

Large IT services firms

Companies such as TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and HCLTech usually hire in large numbers through:

  • Online test: aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal ability and one or two coding questions
  • Technical interview: your projects, OOPs, one language in depth, DBMS, basic DSA
  • HR interview: communication, attitude, relocation and shift flexibility
Product companies

Product and tech-first companies

Companies such as Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Adobe, Flipkart and fast-growing startups usually run:

  • Online assessment: two or three DSA problems, timed
  • Two to four technical rounds: live coding and problem solving
  • Design or project round: low-level design or a deep dive into a project
  • Hiring manager or behavioral round: teamwork, ownership and culture fit
02 · Preparation

Before the interview

  1. Know your resume line by line. Anything you've written can be asked. If you can't explain it for two minutes, remove it.
  2. Prepare a 60–90 second introduction. Name, education, your strongest skill, one project and why you're interested in this role. Practice it aloud until it sounds natural, not memorized.
  3. Research the company. What it builds, who its customers are, recent news, and the role's tech stack. Have one reason why you want to work there.
  4. Revise the core subjects. OOPs, DBMS and SQL, operating systems, computer networks and your main language. Use the Practice page for each.
  5. Practice DSA under time. Solve problems with a 30–45 minute timer while explaining your thinking aloud.
  6. Prepare five STAR stories. A team project, a conflict, a failure, a time you led, and a time you learned something fast. Each one should work for several questions.
  7. Do at least two mock interviews. With a friend, a senior or your trainer. Ask for honest feedback.
  8. Sleep properly the night before. A rested mind solves problems faster than a late-night revision.
03 · Technique

Cracking the coding round

Interviewers judge how you think as much as whether you reach the answer. Follow these steps for every coding question.

1

Listen and clarify

Repeat the problem in your own words. Ask about input size, edge cases, duplicates and negative numbers.

2

Work an example

Write a small input and the expected output. This catches misunderstandings early.

3

Say the brute force

Describe the simplest working idea and its time complexity, even if it's slow.

4

Improve it

Look for repeated work. Think hashing, sorting, two pointers, binary search or DP. Explain the trade-off.

5

Code it cleanly

Use clear variable names and small functions. Keep talking while you write.

6

Test and analyze

Dry-run your example and an edge case. State the final time and space complexity.

If you get stuck

  • Say what you know and where you're stuck. Silence is worse than a partial idea.
  • Try a smaller version of the problem.
  • Take hints gracefully and build on them. Using a hint well is a good sign.

Theory questions

  • Start with a one-line definition, then give an example.
  • If you don't know, say so honestly and explain how you'd find out.
  • Connect answers to your projects where you can.
04 · HR

Acing the HR round

Use STAR

Situation, Task, Action, Result. Spend most of the time on what you did and end with a measurable result or lesson.

Be honest and positive

Don't invent stories or speak badly about a college, teacher or past employer. Turn weaknesses into what you're doing to improve.

Know your answers on logistics

Relocation, shifts, service agreements and joining dates. Decide your answers before the interview so you don't hesitate.

05 · Presence

Communication and body language

Do
  • Greet the panel with a smile and wait to be asked to sit
  • Sit upright with your hands relaxed on the table
  • Keep natural eye contact with everyone on the panel
  • Speak clearly and at a steady pace
  • Pause to think. "Let me think about that for a moment" is fine
  • Thank the panel at the end
Don't
  • Interrupt the interviewer
  • Fold your arms, slouch or fidget with a pen
  • Fill silence with "umm" and "like"
  • Check your phone, even on silent
  • Bluff when you don't know something
  • Argue if you disagree with feedback
06 · Dress code

What to wear

Formal and simple. Your clothes should be clean, ironed and comfortable, so the panel remembers your answers, not your outfit.

Men

  • Formal shirt in a light solid color: white, light blue or light gray
  • Dark dress pants: navy, black or charcoal
  • Leather belt matching your shoes
  • Polished formal shoes with dark socks
  • Tie and blazer if the company is formal or the club dress code asks for it
  • Neat haircut; clean shave or a neatly trimmed beard

Women

  • Formal shirt with dress pants or a knee-length skirt
  • Solid or subtle colors
  • Closed formal shoes or low heels you can walk in comfortably
  • Minimal jewelry and light makeup
  • Hair neatly tied or set away from the face

Everyone

  • Avoid jeans, T-shirts, sneakers, loud prints and big logos
  • Trim and clean your nails
  • Use a mild deodorant; skip strong perfume
  • Carry a simple folder for documents
  • Try the full outfit the day before
  • For online interviews, dress formally on top and keep a plain background
07 · Remote

Online interviews

Set up

  • Test the meeting platform, camera and microphone the day before
  • Use a wired connection or sit close to the router; keep mobile data as backup
  • Charge your laptop and keep the charger plugged in
  • Put the camera at eye level with light in front of you
  • Choose a quiet room and tell your family the time

During

  • Join five minutes early
  • Look at the camera when speaking, not your own video
  • Keep an IDE and a notepad open, and close other tabs and notifications
  • If the connection drops, rejoin quickly and say what you were explaining
  • Share your screen only when asked

    09 · Avoid

    Mistakes to avoid

    Jumping straight into code

    Clarify and explain your approach first. Code written in a rush usually has to be rewritten.

    Adding skills you can't defend

    A long skills list invites hard questions. Three skills you know well beat ten you've only heard of.

    Memorized answers

    Interviewers can tell. Know your key points and say them in your own words.

    Not asking any questions

    Always have two or three questions ready. It shows interest.

    Arriving late

    Reach 30 minutes early for in-person interviews. Plan your route the day before.

    Giving up on a hard question

    Partial progress with clear reasoning often scores better than a correct answer with no explanation.

    10 · Follow-up

    After the interview

    Do this the same day

    • Write down every question you were asked
    • Note what went well and what to improve
    • Send a short thank-you email if you have the interviewer's or HR contact's address
    • Solve any problem you couldn't finish, so you're ready next time
